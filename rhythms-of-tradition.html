Rhythms of Tradition

Rumana Rahman Acrylic, 76 × 60 cm

About the Series

Deeply rooted in the textures of everyday life in Bangladesh, Rumana Rahman is drawn to the objects, symbols, and stories that shape the country's cultural landscape. In Rhythms of Tradition she revisits icons like the Elisha fish and the gamcha, not to preserve them in their traditional forms, but to explore how they live within her today — symbols that have travelled with her from childhood memories of riverbank markets and handwoven cloth drying in the sun, to the layered chaos of contemporary city life.

Through acrylic's fast-drying intensity and layering potential, she translates memory into motion, tradition into abstraction. The Elisha fish becomes movement, rhythm, fragility; the gamcha, once wrapped around the body or used in daily labour, becomes a grid of identity — intersecting lines that speak to belonging, resilience, and transformation. Rather than nostalgia, these paintings are an ongoing conversation with heritage, where the past is neither fixed nor lost, but alive and constantly being reshaped.
